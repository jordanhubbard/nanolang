# v5.1 compiler fixed points

I qualified both compiler execution routes from exact candidate
`ff184c0ef7fe3fd378a2f3c8cf7214d9bf54de96`. Each route compares raw
modules from its own successive compiler generations. I do not normalize
paths, rewrite imports, or omit the declared host closure.

The final release commit may follow this pin with interpreter, CI, roadmap,
and presentation changes. Those changes do not alter the compiler source used
by either retained fixed-point run.

  `d7b622ef4de7add8f78d06c600342ac4c8a44f8af6196e67f0c6ab0746e1b15a`.
  I record it separately because it comes from a different lowering

## Standalone native route

- The native Stage 1 and Stage 2 modules are each 853,756 bytes. Both have
  SHA-256
  `314fb98e79220454ac47e6bf877bc078cfb69026b06b293dac8533cb54c5f973`.
- Stage 1 generation took 1,957.672 seconds and Stage 2 took 1,956.481
  seconds. The recovery supervisor used separate 7,200-second ceilings to
  preserve either terminal; both generations completed within the 3,600-second
  release allowance. Both modules verified.
- The exact five-library host closure remained equal across Stage 1 and Stage
  2. It contains `compiler_support`, `file_companion`,
  `file_source_catalog`, `nanoisa`, and `std`.
- I translated the retained VM module to a 24,399,962-byte C product in
  226.269 seconds and compiled it with `-O1` in 20.359 seconds. Stage 1
  translation took 220.371 seconds, and its optimized native build took
  19.372 seconds. Peak owned RSS was 7,120,288 KiB, below the 48 GiB bound.
- The Stage 1 native compiler compiled the unchanged hello source; the result
  verified and executed with `Hello from NanoLang!`. Neither native compiler
  links `nano_vm`.
- The VM Stage 2 seed has the same 853,756-byte size but different raw bytes
  and a different absolute private-library closure. I record that expected
  seed-to-native transition without normalization. The native Stage 1 and
  Stage 2 dumps and their five-library paths and hashes are byte-identical.
- The original supervisor retained both generations, then exited at its
  deliberately preserved but over-strong seed-closure assertion before hello.
  Its manifest and post-run integrity hashes are
  `21a2daeb161882fa128d46c2561536bdf3f841fab0bad619cf0158bc0322fceb`
  and `b36cba5f35fa9adb1902c00a9a72ebc7c3edbc55bb30923cd415132d6da8c037`.
  A separate read-only acceptance receipt reverified both modules, compared
  the native closure, compiled and ran hello, and rehashed all sixteen source,
  tool, provider and evidence inputs without changing the original directory.
  Its SHA-256 is
  `0673ab493e91f5ea4c134269b3a84dc40e78b1dc9983572f6bcbd7e6df07e74f`.

These fixed points establish reproducible compiler artifacts for the tested
routes and host closure. They do not prove compiler correctness.
