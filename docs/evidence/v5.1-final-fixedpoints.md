# v5.1 compiler fixed points

I qualified both compiler execution routes from the same compiler-source pin,
`ebe3afddc8c9a7cd89b5d64b2928ea9dd269d08c`. Each route compares its own raw
Stage 1 and Stage 2 modules. I do not compare the VM route with the native
route: their host paths are different and their serialized modules are not the
same size.

## NanoVM route

- Stage 1 and Stage 2 are each 491,788 bytes.
- Both have SHA-256
  `7b8f96e51a146364448a37cce43dd414734d31aad53eb57359c6768a107f1575`.
- Generation took 380.815 seconds and 385.655 seconds.
- Both modules verified. Stage 2 compiled, verified and executed the unchanged
  hello source.
- The declared three-library host closure and post-run source/tool hashes were
  unchanged. The complete gate took 821.266 seconds.
- The retained raw log has SHA-256
  `595dde70a33fba603759d5ed320ad7dad470afa6d8109b2c85a567fdd461826c`.

## Standalone native route

The native route first translated the seed module through `nvm2c`, built a
standalone compiler that does not link `nano_vm`, and used that process for the
two full compiler generations.

- Stage 1 and Stage 2 are each 491,800 bytes.
- Both have SHA-256
  `c3a425bb90edc4101a1e7c1ec92f2acee1a781a30407d608cfc381ea3a13c2b4`.
- Generation took 1,238.087 seconds and 1,286.890 seconds under the unchanged
  1,800-second stage bounds.
- Peak owned RSS was 4,410,608 KiB and 4,796,484 KiB, below the 48 GiB bound.
- Both modules verified. The Stage 1 native compiler compiled the unchanged
  hello source; the result verified and executed.
- The exact `compiler_support`, `nanoisa`, and `std` host-library closure was
  equal across generations. Post-run source, helper, translator, host-library,
  and initial-native hashes were unchanged.
- The retained raw log has SHA-256
  `bc51c6a89d295f0563408839c3f7fd73e763d5241f72a35e0966442441bdec4f`.

These fixed points establish reproducible compiler artifacts for the two
tested routes and host closure. They do not prove compiler correctness or
cross-route byte identity.
